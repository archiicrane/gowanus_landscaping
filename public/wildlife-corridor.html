Ecological Analysis

Wildlife Corridor Feasibility

Can Gowanus serve as a stepping-stone habitat node connecting the green spaces of South Brooklyn? This analysis maps target species across six nearby parks and evaluates which animals could colonize a rewilded Gowanus corridor - and which already exist in parks on both sides.

01 · Context

The Stepping-Stone Problem

Gowanus sits in a gap between two significant green masses: Prospect Park / Green-Wood Cemetery to the east/south and the Red Hook waterfront to the west. Without a continuous green corridor, wildlife cannot move between these nodes. The canal itself is an ecological barrier - rewilding the surrounding blocks converts the gap into a bridge.

04 · Verdict

Is Gowanus a Good Middle Ground?

Summary assessment of the corridor's ecological potential.

Strong Corridor Candidates

  • Red-tailed Hawk - Already patrols the Gowanus blocks from Green-Wood and Prospect. A rewilded edge with perch trees closes the gap immediately.
  • Eastern Gray Squirrel - Present in all six parks. Any planted street tree extends its range.
  • Monarch Butterfly - Uses flowering corridors. The Gowanus planting plan (milkweed, asters, goldenrod) directly targets its migration route.
  • Gray Catbird - Dense shrub nester. Coffey Park and Carroll Park populations would expand naturally into Gowanus edge plantings.
  • American Robin - Lawn/edge species. Immediate colonizer once soil is unsealed.
  • Yellow Warbler - Already at Coffey Park. Willow and elderberry plantings at the canal edge = direct habitat bridge.

Longer-Term Corridor Goals

  • Eastern Screech-Owl - Present at Prospect Park and Green-Wood. Needs mature hollow trees; box programs can accelerate colonization.
  • Wood Thrush - Interior forest specialist. Gowanus cannot provide interior habitat alone - but it extends the Prospect-Green-Wood forest corridor's functional edge.
  • Red Fox - Only at Prospect Park currently. Long-term: if the full corridor is planted, documented range expansion is plausible (they colonized Greenpoint in 2019).
  • Painted Turtle - Canal water quality is improving post-Superfund. Present at Prospect Lake and Green-Wood ponds; canal edge bioswales with calm pools could support them.
  • Great Blue Heron - Foraging range extends to Gowanus Canal now, but nesting requires tall isolated trees over water. Decades-long goal.
  • Osprey - Active over Red Hook Bay. Canal mouth habitat could serve as foraging extension, but nesting requires dedicated platforms over open water.
